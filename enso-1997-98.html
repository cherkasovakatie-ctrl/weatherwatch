Evidence base: scope, index and data

Primary index: NOAA's Relative Oceanic Niño Index (RONI), official since 1 February 2026 [1] [2]. On RONI the 1997–98 peak is +2.28 (NDJ 1997); on the traditional ONI it is +2.37. The two agree within 0.1 °C for this event, unlike 1982–83 (RONI +2.40, ONI +2.14).

Observing system in 1997. The first strong El Niño observed by the completed TAO/TRITON mooring array (finished in December 1994), TOPEX/Poseidon altimetry (from 1992), satellite microwave winds and drifting buoys [3] [12]. Onset, growth and termination are therefore documented daily along the equator, which allows heat-budget and wave analyses that were impossible for 1982. Argo did not yet exist; the subsurface off the equator and outside the array remains less certain.
