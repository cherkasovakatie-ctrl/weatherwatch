By analyzing observational data and performing Indian and Pacific Ocean pacemaker experiments using a state-of-the-art climate model, we find that a positive IOD (pIOD) can favor both cold and warm sea surface temperature anomalies (SSTA) in the tropical Pacific, in contrast to the previously identified pIOD–El Niño connection. Introduction El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is the dominant mode of interannual climate variability on the planet (McPhaden et al. 2006) (Fig. 1a). It is associated with substantial changes in the ocean–atmosphere coupled system in the tropical Pacific Ocean (Bjerknes 1969). Through the atmospheric bridge, ENSO has prominent climatic impacts around the globe (Wallace and Gutzler 1981). For instance, it has been well recognized that El Niño/La Niña can induce basinwide positive/negative sea surface temperature anomalies (SSTA) in the tropical Indian Ocean (Alexander et al. 2002; Klein et al. 1999).
