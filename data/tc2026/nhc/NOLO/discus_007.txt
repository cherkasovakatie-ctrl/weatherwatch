Infrared and proxy-visible satellite imagery continue to show that 
convection remains displaced to the southeast of the low-level 
center of the depression. Recent 0519 UTC Metop-B and 0541 UTC 
Metop-C scatterometer passes indicate that the center is becoming 
elongated, and stretched out with maximum winds of 28 kt. Therefore, 
the initial intensity is kept at 30 kt for this advisory.
 
The system is moving at an estimated motion of 260/9 kt. A generally 
westward motion is forecast over the next few days as the system is 
steered along the southern edge of a mid-level ridge to its north. 
The latest NHC track forecast was shifted southward once again and 
trends towards the HCCA and simple TVCN consensus aids.
 
The depression remains within a moderate wind shear environment, 
with dry mid-level air entraining into the center. Although shear is 
forecast to decrease in about a day, the models show very little 
strengthening. Toward the end of the forecast period, wind shear is 
expected to increase once again, and the depression will move into 
an even drier, more stable airmass, causing the system to weaken and 
struggle to produce deep convection. The latest NHC forecast calls 
for the system to maintain its current intensity and remain below 
tropical storm strength. The system is forecast to become a 
post-tropical cyclone by 60 h, and  open into a trough by day 5.
