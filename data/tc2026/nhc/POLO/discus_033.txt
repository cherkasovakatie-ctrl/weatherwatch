Hurricane Polo Discussion Number  33
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP172026
200 PM MST Mon Sep 28 2026
 
NOAA Hurricane Hunters investigated Polo this afternoon, finding the 
hurricane was still well organized with a 23 n mi wide eye, though 
the eyewall was open to the southwest and there was a subtle 
increase in the vertical tilt comparing the 1-5 km Tail Doppler 
Radar (TDR) wind data from the aircraft. The highest 700 mb 
flight-level winds were 106 kt in the southeast quadrant, which 
using a typical 0.9 reduction factor would suggest an intensity of 
95 kt. However, the TDR data has also been useful to diagnose the 
intensity of Polo since it samples a larger wind field swath, and 
the final southwest to northeast leg through the hurricane indicated 
0.5 km winds up to 112 kt in the northeast eyewall. Experimental 
reduction data from this TDR analysis in this region reduced these 
boundary layer winds down to 100 kt at 10 meters. Thus, the initial 
intensity remains 100 kt for this advisory.
 
Polo continues to move northeastward, but it is starting to 
accelerate some with an current motion of 40 degrees at 10 kt. This 
general motion should continue over the next day with gradual 
acceleration as the hurricane is captured by a strong shortwave 
trough swinging into the southwestern United States. This track will 
take Polo's core over the Baja California Peninsula this evening 
very close to Puerto San Andresito. The hurricane will then briefly 
move over the Gulf of California overnight, finally making a second 
landfall in northwestern mainland Mexico on Tuesday morning. The NHC 
track forecast is essentially an update from this morning, and is in 
the middle of the tightly clustered guidance. 

Sea-surface temperatures actually increase a bit as Polo approaches 
Baja California Sur. Vertical wind shear, while also increasing, is 
unlikely to get strong enough over the next 3-6 h to cause much 
weakening. Thus, Polo is likely to maintain its current intensity 
and be at or near major hurricane intensity when it makes landfall 
on the west coast of Baja California Sur this evening. Weakening 
will likely begin immediately after it moves over the rugged terrain 
of the peninsula, though Polo is still expected to be a hurricane 
when it makes its second landfall in mainland Mexico on the Gulf of 
California coast. Rapid weakening is expected when Polo moves fully 
inland over the high rugged terrain of northwestern Mexico, and the 
tropical cyclone is expected to degenerate into a trough  before its 
remnants reach the southwestern United States.

However Polo's remnant vorticity will ultimately interact and phase 
with a strong cutoff low digging into the the four corners region of 
the U.S. on Tuesday into Wednesday, contributing to heavy rainfall, 
flash flooding, and gusty winds the southwestern U.S. and southern 
Plains. See products issued by the Weather Prediction Center and 
local National Weather Service offices for more information.
 
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Polo is forecast to make landfall on the west coast of the Baja 
California Peninsula near major hurricane strength this evening. 
Conditions will deteriorate quickly, and residents are urged to seek 
safe shelter.  Hurricane and tropical storm conditions will spread 
inland across Baja California tonight, and into mainland Mexico late 
tonight and early Tuesday. A dangerous storm surge is expected in 
areas of onshore winds. 
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Polo will impact Baja California 
Sur along with southern and central portions of Sonora today into 
Tuesday. This rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and 
mudslides, especially in areas of steep terrain. Moisture from Polo 
will support heavy rainfall and flooding potential across the 
Southwest U.S. into the Southern High Plains late today into 
Tuesday.
 
3. Even after Polo dissipates, its mid-level remnants may produce 
strong gusty winds well inland over the higher terrain of northwest 
Mexico and southern New Mexico and far western Texas. 
  
4. Dangerous surf and rip currents are likely along portions of the 
northwest coast of Mexico and Baja California for the next day or 
so.
