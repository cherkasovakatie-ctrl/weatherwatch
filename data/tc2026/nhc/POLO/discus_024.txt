Hurricane Polo Discussion Number  24
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP172026
800 AM MST Sat Sep 26 2026
 
Infrared satellite imagery shows that the eye of Polo has become 
cloud filled since the previous advisory.  This appears to be a 
result of an increase in shear over the system.  The subjective 
Dvorak intensity estimate from TAFB was unchanged from earlier, 
however objective estimates have decreased and range from about 
125-142 kt.  Therefore, the initial intensity for this advisory has 
been reduced to 135 kt, which could be slightly generous.

Some fluctuations in intensity are still possible today, however 
with the gradually increasing shear, slow weakening seems to be the 
more likely scenario during the next 24 to 48 hours.  After that 
time, a more significant increase in southwesterly shear is expected 
which is likely to cause an additional decrease in wind speed as 
Polo approaches the Baja Peninsula.  The latest NHC intensity 
forecast is a blend of the typically reliable HFIP corrected 
consensus and Google Deep Mind aids.  Regardless of the exact 
intensity forecast, Polo is expected to be a strong hurricane when 
it reaches Baja California Sur.
 
The initial motion estimate is west-northwestward, or 295 degrees at 
9 kt.  There is once again no significant change to the track 
forecast reasoning.  Polo is expected to turn northwestward later 
today, and then northward on Sunday as it moves around the western 
periphery of a mid-level ridge over Mexico. By Monday, a mid-level 
trough approaching the west coast of the United States should cause 
Polo to turn northeastward.  This motion is expected to take the 
cyclone over the Baja Peninsula and into northwestern Mexico late 
Monday through Tuesday.  While the track guidance is in generally 
good agreement on the overall scenario, there are some large 
difference in the forward speed of Polo after the northeastward 
turn, which results in a large spread as to when the storm reaches 
the Baja Peninsula. The NHC forecast is along the faster side of the 
guidance with the typically better performing HFIP corrected 
consensus aid and the Google Deep Mind ensemble mean. 
  
Distant moisture from Hurricane Polo and Odalys will be transported
into the southwestern United States this weekend and early next
week, and may indirectly contribute to a heavy rainfall and flash
flood threat in the region. See products issued by the Weather
Prediction Center for more information.
 
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Polo is forecast to approach the west coast of the Baja
California peninsula on Monday as a hurricane, and a Hurricane
Watch is in effect for that region. Tropical storm conditions
could reach portions of the coast as soon as Sunday night or early 
Monday, making further preparations difficult. A storm surge will 
be possible, especially in areas of onshore winds.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Polo may begin to impact Baja 
California Sur late Sunday into Monday. This rainfall may produce 
life-threatening flooding and mudslides, especially in areas of 
steep terrain.
 
3. Dangerous surf and rip currents are likely along portions of
the northwest coast of Mexico and Baja California for the next
several days.
