NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026

Trudy appears to be undergoing a period of rapid intensification.
After the previous advisory, there were several microwave images
that showed the hurricane has developed an eyewall, with a cyan ring
noted on the 37 GHz channel of the 1037 UTC GMI and more recent 1250
UTC WSF-M pass. This signature is often a harbinger or indicates
that rapid intensification is underway. On geostationary satellite
Trudy has been maintaining a healthy central dense overcast with
cold cloud tops between -75 to -80 C with new bursts of convection
fully wrapping around the circulation as it becomes more symmetric.
The subjective Dvorak estimate at 12 UTC was T4.5/77 kt, but given
the improving structure seen on microwave imagery, the initial
intensity is being set at 80 kt this advisory, which is on the high
end of the intensity estimates. This indicates that Trudy has
intensified 30 kt in the past 24 h, qualifying as rapid
intensification. An Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft is
en route to sample the hurricane and should provide critial in-situ
data to help refine the forecast this afternoon.
 
Trudy is now estimated to be moving slowly north-northwestward, or
335 degrees at 5 kt. Over the next 24-36 h Trudy is expected to
maintain this heading but at a slow pace as its steering is
maintained by two ridges, one to its east and another to its north.
These features are split by a mid- to upper-level trough that is
draped across the Yucatan Peninsula, weakening the overall
steering flow. How these synoptic features interact over the next
several days is leading to a lot of track uncertainty over the next
few days, and there are some track solutions (GFS, ECMWF) that show
Trudy making landfall. The latest NHC track forecast has been
shifted eastward compared to the prior one, following the trends in
the guidance. This track is blending the latest HFIP Corrected
Consensus Approach (HCCA), Google DeepMind ensemble mean (GDMI) and
also places a little weight on the latest ECMWF track forecast.
With that said, given the spread of the deterministic and ensemble
track guidance this cycle, this forecast is lower confidence than
usual.
 
Now that Trudy has developed an inner-core, intensification is
likely to be rapid in the short-term. Environmental conditions are
quite favorable (shear under 10 kt, sea-surface temperatures 28-29
C, and abundant moisture) and SHIPS-RI indices are elevated for a
30-40 kt increase in intensity over the next 24 h. Thus, the first
part of this intensity forecast was raised from the previous
advisory, showing Trudy becoming a major hurricane in 12-24 h, and
peaking as a 125 kt Category 4 hurricane in 36 h. This part of the
intensity forecast is on the upper end of the guidance. Thereafter,
both the GFS and ECMWF based SHIPS guidance shows southwesterly
vertical wind shear increasing markedly, and this will also be the
period where Trudy nears the coast of Mexico, where terrain-induced
effects will likely start to affect the circulation. These combined
effects are now forecast to lead to weakening beginning in 48 h and
continuing through the rest of the forecast. One big caveat with
this intensity forecast is just how close Trudy gets to Mexico. A
track closer to the coast may result in much more weakening than
shown here, while a track further offshore may preserve Trudy's
intensity, similar to what we saw with Simon earlier this week. The
second part of the NHC intensity forecast falls back closer to the
guidance mean. Given the proximity of Trudy's track to Mexico, this
is also a lower confidence intensity forecast after the next 48 h.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Trudy is forecast to be a major hurricane as it approaches the
coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico this weekend. There is
an increasing risk of wind, rain, and storm surge impacts. A
Tropical Storm Watch has now been issued for a portion of the
southwestern Mexican coast, and additional watches will likely be
required further up the coast later today.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Trudy will impact the southwest
Mexican coast between Guerrero and Jalisco through the weekend. This
rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides by the
weekend across Colima and Jalisco, especially in areas along steep
terrain.
 
3. Large swells will begin to affect portions of the coast of
southwestern Mexico Friday and continue through the weekend. These
swells could cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
