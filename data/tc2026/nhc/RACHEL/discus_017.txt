Hurricane Rachel Discussion Number  17
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP182026
200 AM MST Thu Oct 01 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that Rachel's convective structure has
improved over the past several hours. Deep convection continues to
expand over the center, with cloud-top temperatures as cold as -85
C. The latest subjective Dvorak intensity estimate from TAFB is
T4.5/77 kt, while objective estimates have ranged from 65 to 85 kt.
Given these estimates and the improved convective organization, the
initial intensity is raised to 85 kt.
 
Rachel continues to move west-northwestward, or 300/5 kt, and this
motion is expected to continue during the next day or so while a
mid- to upper-level trough remains over the southwestern United
States. As the trough lifts northeastward and weakens, a ridge is
forecast to build north of Rachel, causing the system to gradually
turn more toward the west by the weekend. The track guidance remains
in good agreement on this overall scenario, and the latest NHC track
forecast is essentially unchanged from the previous prediction,
lying near the HCCA and GDMI aids.
 
Environmental conditions should remain generally favorable for
strengthening during the next couple of days, with very warm sea
surface temperatures and plenty of mid-level moisture along the
forecast track. Persistent easterly to northeast vertical wind shear
of around 15 kt may be a limiting factor in the short term, but the
shear is forecast to gradually relax thereafter. Rachel should
continue to strengthen over the next 24 h, especially if the
inner-core structure continues to improve, and is forecast to become
a major hurricane within the next couple of days. Thereafter,
progressively cooler sea surface temperatures and dry mid-level air
should induce a gradual weakening trend. The latest NHC intensity
forecast is similar to the previous advisory, lying above the
guidance envelope in the short term before trending toward the upper
end of the guidance thereafter.
