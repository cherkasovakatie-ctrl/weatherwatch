Tropical Storm Rachel Discussion Number  49
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP182026

Satellite imagery indicates that Rachel's convective organization 
remains limited, with the deepest convection confined to the 
northern semicircle. The latest subjective Dvorak intensity 
estimate from TAFB was T3.0/45 kt, which is in good agreement with 
the latest objective estimates from UW-CIMSS. Given these data and 
the limited convective organization, the initial intensity is 
lowered to 45 kt.

Rachel has begun its anticipated northeastward turn over the past
several hours, with an initial motion estimated at 045/3 kt. A
northeastward motion with a significant increase in forward speed
is expected during the next couple of days as the cyclone
interacts with a deepening mid- to upper-level trough over the
western United States. Track guidance remains in good agreement
and depicts Rachel moving ashore over the northern Baja California
Peninsula late Saturday before continuing northeastward into the
southwestern United States on Sunday. The latest NHC track
forecast is largely unchanged from the previous prediction and
lies close to the HCCA and GDMI aids.

Despite the very dry and stable air and relatively cool sea surface 
temperatures along the forecast track, some of the dynamical 
guidance suggests that Rachel could maintain tropical storm strength 
through landfall this weekend. As Rachel accelerates northeastward 
and interacts with the deepening trough, increasing upper-level 
forcing and improving mid-level moisture could support intermittent 
bursts of deep convection. Although this interaction will also 
result in a significant increase in vertical wind shear, which 
should further disrupt the cyclone's tropical structure, some 
strengthening is possible during the next 24 to 36 hours. The latest 
NHC intensity forecast shows Rachel reaching 50 kt in 36 h before 
weakening as it moves inland. Rachel is expected to become 
post-tropical over the southwestern United States by 60 h and 
dissipate shortly thereafter. The latest NHC intensity forecast is 
similar to the previous prediction and lies near the middle to upper 
end of the guidance envelope. Given the potential for 
tropical-storm-force winds along portions of northern Baja 
California this weekend, a Tropical Storm Watch will likely be 
required for portions of the northern Baja California Peninsula 
will be likely later today.
 
 
Key Messages:

1. Rachel is expected to approach the coast of northern Baja 
California, Mexico, and the northern Gulf of California at tropical 
storm strength over the weekend, and a Tropical Storm Watch could 
be required for a portion of this area later today.

2. Strong gusty winds associated with the remnants of Rachel are
expected to spread over interior portions of the Southwest United
States, especially in areas of higher terrain, from this weekend
into early next week.

3. Rachel and the moisture associated with the system will spread
over portions of the Southwest United States and northern Baja
California Peninsula through this weekend. This will lead to
increasing coverage of rainfall and the potential for flash and
urban flooding.

4. Swells partly generated by Rachel will continue to build along
the coast of Southern California and northern Baja California.
These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip
current conditions, as well as minor to moderate coastal flooding,
through early next week. Please consult products from your local
weather office.
