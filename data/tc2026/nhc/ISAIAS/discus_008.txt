Hurricane Isaias Discussion Number   8
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       AL092026
1000 AM CDT Thu Oct 08 2026

Isaias is strengthening in an environment of moderate vertical wind 
shear.  Observations from an Air Force Hurricane Hunter aircraft 
indicate that the central pressure continues to fall and the 
maximum winds are now near 75 kt.  The center of the hurricane is 
near the southwestern side of a Central Dense Overcast feature, and 
some convective banding features are evident over the eastern 
portion of the circulation.  Additional aircraft reconnaissance 
missions are scheduled for today, tonight, and Friday, so we will 
have close monitoring of the intensity of the system up until 
landfall.

Center fixes from the Hurricane Hunters indicate that Isais 
continues on a generally east-northward track with an initial 
motion of about 060/9 kt.  During the next 36 hours, the hurricane 
should gradually turn toward the northeast and north as it moves 
between a ridge extending from the Bahamas across the Florida 
Peninsula and a zonally-oriented mid-level trough near the 
northwestern Gulf coast.  On the forecast track, the center of the 
hurricane is forecast to move across the east-central to 
northeastern Gulf coast in 36-42 hours.  Thereafter, the weakening 
cyclone should move over the southeastern United States bringing 
strong winds well inland.  The track guidance has not changed much 
for this advisory cycle and the official forecast is similar to that 
of the previous advisory.

The intensity forecast up to landfall is somewhat challenging.  
On the one hand, the hurricane should move over waters of high 
oceanic heat content and within a fairly moist air mass through 
tomorrow.  On the other hand, the SHIPS model input parameters show 
a significant increase in vertical wind shear, particularly when 
Isais nears the coast.  Regarding, the shorter-term intensity 
forecast, a recent WSFM microwave satellite image at 37 GHz 
frequency shows a closed ring around the eye.  This is typically a 
harbinger of rapid intensification.  The 12-hour forecast does show 
a relatively large increase in strength, but in view of the 
expected stronger shear in 24-36 hours, some decrease in intensity 
is indicated as Isaias approaches the coast.  Regardless, Isaias is 
expected to remain a dangerous hurricane through landfall, and 
gusty winds are expected well inland after landfall given the 
anticipated expansion of the wind field and accelerating forward 
speed of the storm.
 
Key Messages:
 
1. A life-threatening storm surge is expected from the Mouth of the 
Mississippi River to Suwannee, Florida. Residents in the warning 
area should follow advice given by local officials and evacuate 
immediately if told to do so.

2. Damaging hurricane force-winds are expected over portions of the 
Florida Panhandle, southern Alabama, and southern Mississippi where 
a Hurricane Warning is in effect. Residents in these areas should 
prepare for the possibility of long-duration power outages. 

3. Damaging wind gusts are possible over much of central and 
northern Alabama and portions of western and northern Georgia, 
especially in areas of higher terrain.  Additional Tropical Storm 
and High Wind Watches and Warnings will likely be required for 
portions of that area later today. Residents should secure loose 
outdoor items, avoid driving high-profile vehicles, and prepare for 
power outages.

4. Isaias will bring areas of heavy rainfall to the central U.S. 
Gulf coast and much of the Southeast Friday into the weekend. 
Considerable flash and urban flooding, along with significant river 
flooding, will be possible.

5. Isaias could bring gusty winds and areas of heavy rain to the 
western portion of the Yucatan Peninsula today.
