Tropical Storm Isaias Discussion Number   3
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       AL092026
400 AM CDT Wed Oct 07 2026
 
Satellite imagery depicts a bursting pattern with the tropical 
cyclone this morning, with cloud top temperatures below -85 C in the 
deep convection close to the estimated center. Earlier, an 0307 UTC 
ASCAT-C pass suggested the low-level circulation was still rather 
broad, with a peak wind retrieval of only 31 kt. However, the 
bursting convection already appears to be tightening up the 
circulation somewhat, with a nearby surface observation from a buoy 
north of the center recently reporting a sustained 10 m adjusted 
wind of 35 kt. This information is enough to upgrade the depression 
to Tropical Storm Isaias for this advisory. This initial intensity 
will be 35 kt, which also agrees with the 06 UTC Dvorak fix of 
T2.5/35 kt. Both Air Force Reserve and NOAA Hurricane Hunter 
reconnaissance missions are scheduled to sample the tropical storm 
later today. 
 
Isaias is estimated to be moving east-northeastward this morning at 
075/7 kt. This general motion should continue today as the storm's 
steering is influenced by a zonally oriented mid-level trough 
currently draped along the northern Gulf coast. Over time, this 
trough is expected to be reinforced by another short-wave that is 
moving across the Rocky Mountains, with the trough ultimately 
sharpening up northwest of Isaias over Texas. At the same time, a 
subtropical ridge centered south of Bermuda is expected to shift 
westward, helping to enhance the deep-layer southerly flow over the 
eastern Gulf. Thus, Isaias is expected to turn northeastward and 
then northward on Thursday and Friday, making its approach to the 
northern Gulf coast in 60-72 h. As mentioned in the previous 
discussion, there remains quite a bit of along and across-track 
spread in the track guidance, with the AI-model guidance remaining 
on the east side, and physics-based models on the west side of the 
guidance envelope. The NHC track forecast continues to favor more of 
the eastward solutions, but not quite as far east as the latest HFIP 
Corrected Consensus Approach (HCCA) or Google DeepMind ensemble mean 
(GDMI). This latest track is a eastward shift compared to the 
previous forecast, but given large amount of across- and along-track 
spread users should continue to monitor forecast updates given the 
below-average track confidence.

The short-term environment near Isaias is rather favorable, with 
very warm 30-31 C sea-surface temperatures, plenty of deep-layer 
moisture, with light to moderate shear diagnosed between 10-15 kt by 
SHIPS guidance. While it is difficult to get an exact gauge on the 
tropical storm's core structure since there have not been any 
recent microwave images, it is worth noting both the 00 UTC HAFS-A/B 
runs appeared to initialize with a broad structure similar to what 
was noted in the earlier ASCAT pass. Despite their initial broad 
structure, these hurricane-regional models show Isaias developing an 
inner-core rather quickly later today. There was a notable increase 
in the intensity aid guidance this forecast cycle, and the NHC 
intensity forecast is being raised accordingly, now showing Isaias 
reaching a peak intensity of 95 kt in 48 h while it is over the 
central Gulf. Vertical wind shear then increases quite dramatically 
in the final 24 hours before the cyclone reaches the northern Gulf 
coast, though it is looking more likely that Isaias will be a 
significant hurricane when it reaches the coast. Near landfall, 
Isaias will also be interacting with the short-wave trough, likely 
leading to an expanding wind field. Additional convective and 
motion asymmetries will likely cause rain and wind impacts to 
extend far from the center on the eastern side of the storm.
 
Key Messages:
 
1. There is an increasing risk of dangerous wind and storm surge 
impacts along portions of the northern Gulf Coast. Residents from 
Louisiana to the Florida Panhandle should closely monitor the latest 
forecast updates and ensure they have their hurricane plan in place. 
Hurricane and Storm Surge Watches will likely be required for a 
portion of the northern Gulf coast later today. 
 
2. Tropical Storm Isaias will bring areas of heavy rainfall to the 
central U.S. Gulf coast and much of the Southeast Friday into the 
weekend. Flash and urban flooding, along with isolated river 
flooding, will be possible.
 
3. Yucatan Peninsula of Mexico: Tropical Storm Isaias is expected to
strengthen and could bring gusty winds and areas of heavy rain to
the western portion of the Yucatan Peninsula over the next day or 
two.
