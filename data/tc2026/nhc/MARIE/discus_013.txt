Marie has numerous bands of deep convection in its circulation, but 
the inner core is still not very well defined with a somewhat 
ragged-looking eye.  The system has a prominent upper-level outflow 
pattern, indicative of a low-shear environment.  Remotely-sensed 
intensity estimates are generally in the range of 80-90 kt, and the 
current intensity estimate is kept at 85 kt for this advisory.

The hurricane is moving west-northwestward, or around 295/8 kt, on 
the southwestern periphery of a mid-tropospheric ridge.  Global 
models depict a 500-mb trough swinging into California in a couple 
of days.  This evolution is likely to result in Marie turning 
toward the northwest in 36 hours or so.  Later in the forecast 
period, the weakening cyclone should move more with the low- to 
mid-level environmental flow and remain well offshore over the 
eastern Pacific.  The official track forecast is nudged a bit to 
the right of the previous one in 3-5 days, toward the latest Google 
DeepMind and corrected consensus, HCCA, guidance.

Marie is expected to remain in an environment of low vertical wind 
shear for the next several days.  However SSTs are forecast to 
decrease, and the system should be encounter a drier low- to 
mid-level air mass by 48 hours.  Some strengthening is forecast 
while the hurricane remains in a conducive atmospheric and oceanic 
environment through tonight.  Later, a weakening trend is likely as 
Marie encounters less favorable conditions.  The official forecast 
is close to the intensity model consensus.
