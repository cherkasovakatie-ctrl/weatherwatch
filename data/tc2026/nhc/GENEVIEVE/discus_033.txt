Tropical Storm Genevieve Discussion Number  33
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP072026
1100 PM HST Fri Jul 31 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that Genevieve’s convective coverage has 
diminished over the past few hours as it continues
west-northwestward through a dry and stable environment. A 0551Z 
ASCAT-B pass, however, showed that the circulation remains 
well-defined and sampled a peak wind of 40 kt north of the 
circulation. This aligns well with the latest intensity estimates 
from TAFB and UW-CIMSS. Based on these data, the initial intensity 
is held at 45 kt. 

The initial motion estimate is west-northwestward, or 280/11 kt. 
This general motion will continue over the weekend as a low- to 
mid-level ridge remains north of the cyclone. Thereafter, a gradual 
turn to the northwest, then north-northwest is forecast due to an 
upper low located to its north over the far northeastern Pacific. 
The track guidance remains tightly clustered through the weekend, 
with some cross-track spread depicted early next week as it turns 
north-northwest. The updated NHC track forecast is essentially 
unchanged from the previous advisory and closely follows the 
multi-model consensus (HCCA).

Guidance favors a gradual weakening trend as Genevieve remains in a 
dry and stable environment with progressively cooler sea-surface 
temperatures along its track. Additionally, increasing southwesterly 
shear is forecast to begin by the end of the weekend. These 
conditions will result in further weakening, with Genevieve expected 
to degenerate into a post-tropical cyclone by Sunday night. The 
updated NHC intensity forecast reflects this trend and is very 
similar to the previous advisory.
