Tropical Storm Lowell Discussion Number  15
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM HST Sun Aug 30 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that the convective coverage associated 
with Lowell has changed little over the past several hours. 
Persistent easterly vertical wind shear continues to displace the 
bulk of the deep convection over and west of the low-level 
circulation center. Subjective Dvorak intensity estimates from HFO 
and JTWC are 55 kt and 45 kt, respectively, while the latest 
objective intensity estimates range from about 40 to 50 kt. A blend 
of these estimates supports increasing the initial intensity to 50 
kt.

The initial motion is 270/10 kt. A low- to mid-level ridge north of 
the cyclone should remain the primary steering influence during the 
next couple of days, resulting in a continued westward motion. The 
track guidance remains in relatively good agreement through about 60 
h. Thereafter, considerable cross-track spread persists among the 
guidance as Lowell gradually slows and begins to turn 
west-northwestward. Some of this spread may be due to the potential 
influence of Hurricane Karina to the northeast on Lowell's steering 
flow, as well as differences in how the models depict a mid- to 
upper-level trough northwest of the Hawaiian Islands and the 
resulting weakness in the subtropical ridge. The HCCA and Google 
DeepMind ensemble mean remain along the southern side of the 
guidance envelope through day 5, while several other models favor a 
more northwestward track along the northern side of the guidance 
envelope. The latest NHC track forecast is essentially an update of 
the previous advisory and remains near the HCCA, Google DeepMind 
ensemble mean, and simple consensus (TVCN).

Moderate easterly vertical wind shear continues to affect Lowell and 
should temper the rate of strengthening in the short term. The shear 
is forecast to decrease during the next 12 to 24 h while the cyclone 
remains over warm waters, which should allow Lowell to become better 
organized and continue strengthening. A faster rate of 
intensification is forecast between 24 and 72 h as environmental 
conditions become more favorable. Thereafter, the intensity is 
forecast to level off around 96 h. The NHC intensity forecast is 
essentially an update of the previous forecast.

 
Key Messages:
 
1. Lowell is forecast to move generally westward and could be in the
vicinity of the Hawaiian Islands late this week. Interests in Hawaii
should monitor the progress of this storm.
