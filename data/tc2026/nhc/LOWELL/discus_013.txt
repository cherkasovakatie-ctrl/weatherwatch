Tropical Storm Lowell Discussion Number  13
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Sun Aug 30 2026
 
Lowell's outflow to the east is starting to improve, a sign that the 
persistent easterly shear might be decreasing. However, this has not 
yet resulted in a noticeable increase in the convective 
organization, as the center is still located just under the eastern 
side of a triangular-shaped convective mass.  A just-received 
ASCAT-C overpass shows wind vectors of 42 kt in the northwest 
quadrant. Based on this and the various satellite intensity 
estimates, the initial intensity remains 45 kt.
 
The initial motion is now 270/11 kt. As before, a low- to mid-level 
ridge north of the cyclone should remain the primary steering 
influence during the next couple of days, resulting in a continued 
generally westward motion. After that, there remains significant 
cross-track spread in the guidance. Some of this may be due to the 
possible influence of Hurricane Karina on Lowell's steering. The two 
cyclones are likely to stay far enough apart that direct interaction 
is unlikely. However, the Canadian model forecast still suggests the 
possibility of Karina affecting Lowell's motion.  Another cause for 
the spread may be the models' differences in the forecast of a mid- 
to upper-level trough northwest of the Hawaiian Islands.  The GFS 
forecasts the trough develop farther south and create a weakness in 
the subtropical ridge with Lowell moving farther north, while the 
ECMWF keeps the trough farther to the north with less weakening of 
the ridge and Lowell staying farther south. Currently, it is 
difficult to tell which of these scenarios will become reality. The 
new forecast track lies near the HFIP Corrected Consensus model and 
is similar to the previous track.

The intensity guidance is unchanged since the last advisory, and 
indeed it hasn't changed much for the past two days.  Lowell should 
start intensifying in the next 12-24 h, with a faster rate of 
strengthening between 24-72 h as conditions become more favorable. 
The guidance suggests the intensity should level off around 96-120 
h, and the forecast follows this. The new intensity forecast is an 
update of the previous forecast.
