Hurricane Odalys Discussion Number  25
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP162026
800 PM PDT Fri Sep 25 2026
 
Odalys remains a powerful major hurricane, though satellite imagery 
is beginning to show signs of weakening. The eye of Odalys remains 
ragged, embedded within deep convection with cloud top temperatures 
around -70 to -75 C. The latest subjective Dvorak intensity CI 
number from TAFB was 6.0/115 kt but the final-T number was 5.5/102 
kt. This generally aligns with most UW-CIMSS objective satellite 
intensity estimates, which range from 98 to 106 kt. Based on a blend 
of these data, the initial intensity is lowered to 105 kt.
 
The initial motion is estimated to be northward, or 010/4 kt, 
continues to be steered by the flow between a mid- to upper-level 
trough to its west and a mid-level ridge over northern Mexico. The 
hurricane is forecast to continue on this trajectory over the next 
day or so as the aforementioned trough amplifies over the eastern 
Pacific. By early next week, Odalys is anticipated to abruptly turn 
to the east and southeast as the increasingly shallow circulation 
becomes steered by the low-level flow. The latest track guidance 
closely follows the reliable HCCA aid, pulls the track slightly 
farther west ahead of the sharp, easterly turn, and mirrors the 
previous advisory for the remainder of the forecast period.
 
Over the weekend, Odalys is expected to encounter a progressively 
hostile environment, marked by moderate to strong vertical wind 
shear and an increase in mid-level dry air. These conditions will 
likely make it difficult for Odalys to sustain organized convection 
later this weekend, and the updated forecast shows Odalys as a 
post-tropical cyclone at 60 h based on the latest GFS and ECMWF 
simulated satellite imagery.
 
Distant moisture from Hurricane Odalys and Polo will be transported 
into the southwestern United States this weekend and early next 
week, and may indirectly contribute to a heavy rainfall and flash 
flood threat in the region. See products issued by the Weather 
Prediction Center for more information.
