National charts

Founding dates are historical facts; founding times are conventions, and several are disputed. Each chart carries a rating — A documented time, B well-attested, C disputed, X no time (noon chart). The Moon and the angles are used for transit timing only on A and B charts, because a one-hour error moves the Ascendant by about 15°.

Method and sources

Computation. Positions from PyEphem (VSOP87 planetary theory), apparent geocentric longitudes of date, tropical zodiac. Exact aspect and ingress times are found by root-finding to under a minute. Checked against published values: the Jupiter–Saturn conjunction of 21 Dec 2020 (18:20 UTC, 0°30′ Aquarius), Pluto’s Aquarius ingresses of 2023–24, the Saturn–Neptune conjunction of 20 Feb 2026, the 2024–26 equinox times to the minute, all 17 eclipses of 2024–2027, and the Sibly USA and 1947 India charts. Eclipses are flagged by the Moon’s latitude at new or full moon (any type, including partial and penumbral); total/annular is not computed. Mean lunar node. Cross-check any single date against JPL Horizons or astro.com.

National charts. Dates, places and times as used in the mundane literature (Campion, The Book of World Horoscopes, and the national-chart collections at astro.com, astrologyking.com and starsutra.in). Ratings are this site’s own judgement of how well the time is documented.

World events. A hand-picked list for orientation. Because it was chosen after the fact, it cannot test anything — any hand-picked list will appear to line up with some cycle. The market tests use the whole monthly record instead.
