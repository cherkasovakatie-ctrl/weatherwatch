Claim vs evidence

'95% chance the AMOC collapses': what the number means

Live Updated: 2026-10-30 · All claim-vs-evidence cards

Summary

No study we read says there is a 95 percent chance that the Atlantic overturning circulation (AMOC) collapses before 2100. The widely repeated '95%' is the width of an uncertainty interval around an estimated collapse year in one 2023 paper, which was corrected in 2025 (current text: central estimate 2065, interval 2037-2109). The IPCC has medium confidence that an abrupt collapse will not happen before 2100, while weakening is very likely. Direct measurements at 26 N show strong variability and a decline since about 2010; they are not a forecast.

What is known, forecast and unknown

Known: observation
  • Observed: RAPID 26 N array, 2004-2024, shows a decline since about 2010-11 and strong variability (rapid.ac.uk).
  • Observed: North Atlantic sea-surface temperature records since 1870 (used indirectly in the 2023 paper).
Forecast: not an observation
  • Model-based, not observations: IPCC AR6 (weakening very likely; no abrupt collapse before 2100, medium confidence); 2024-2026 studies disagree on how close a tipping point is.
Unknown
  • Whether sea-surface temperature tracks the AMOC well enough to date a collapse.
  • How close the real AMOC is to a tipping point (models disagree, and may be biased toward stability).
  • The mean RAPID transport in Sverdrups and its trend were not obtained.

What the AMOC is

The AMOC is a system of Atlantic currents that carries warm water north near the surface and cold water south at depth. It is not the same thing as the Gulf Stream, which is wind-driven [a1].

Where '95%' comes from

Ditlevsen and Ditlevsen (2023) used North Atlantic sea-surface temperatures since 1870 as an indirect signal of the AMOC and extrapolated a trend toward a tipping point. In the current text the central estimate is 2065 with a 95 percent confidence interval of 2037-2109 [a2]. A 95 percent interval is a range for the year, not a 95 percent probability of collapse. The paper names no emissions scenario and assumes a steady drift; it says it cannot rule out other mechanisms [a2].

The 2025 correction

In August 2025 the authors corrected a code error that changed the tipping-time estimate by 8 years; figures, a table and estimates in the text were updated [a3]. The earlier coverage quoted about 2057, consistent with that shift, but the notice itself does not list the old and new values.

What other work says

The IPCC's sixth assessment has medium confidence that there will not be an abrupt collapse before 2100, and says weakening is very likely [a4]. A 2024 model study simulated a tipping event and argued the present AMOC is on a tipping course; its methods are debated [a5]. A 2025 Nature study reports weakening of 20-81 percent over 90 years in simulations but no complete collapse [a6]. A 2026 study constrained by observations reports a likely decline of 42-58 percent by 2100 (news report; other experts caution) [a7]. A 2026 preprint is not peer reviewed [a8].

What is observed

The RAPID array at 26 N has measured the overturning since 2004 and shows variability from days to a decade, with a sustained decline after 2010-11 [a9]. The proxy used for the 2023 estimate (sea-surface temperature) is questioned by other scientists [a10].
