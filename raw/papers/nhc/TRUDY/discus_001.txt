Tropical Depression Twenty-One-E Discussion Number   1
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
900 AM CST Tue Oct 13 2026
 
Satellite images show that the area of low pressure located to the 
south of southern Mexico has become better organized over the past 
24 hours.  Large areas of banded deep convection are seen to the 
north and the east of the low-level center.  An OSCAT pass from 0630 
UTC this morning showed a closed circulation, but it was a bit short 
of being considered well-defined at that time since the data still 
showed a broad and somewhat elongated area of weak winds within 
about 75 n mi of the center.  However, the convective organization 
and curved banding has increased since that time, and the 
circulation appears to have tightened up a bit over the past several 
hours.  The subjective Dvorak estimate from TAFB is T2.0/30 kt, and 
it is estimated that a tropical depression has formed with an 
initial intensity of 30 kt.

The initial motion of Tropical Depression Twenty-One-E is estimated 
to be northwestward, or 305/9 kt.  A west-northwestward to 
northwestward motion should continue over the next 36 hours while a 
mid-level ridge persists over the northwestern Gulf of America.  
Thereafter, a turn to the northwest or north-northwest is likely as 
the ridge weakens.  A decrease in forward speed is also expected 
after day 2 as the cyclone gets caught in weaker steering currents 
in between the weak ridge to its north and Hurricane Simon to its 
northwest.  Spread in the track forecast is larger than normal from 
day 3 to 5, and some of this uncertainty is likely also due to 
uncertainty in Simon's track.  A cold front moving southward from 
Texas into Mexico toward the end of the forecast period could help 
draw Simon closer to the coastline of Mexico, if the front is potent 
enough.  Regardless, Tropical Depression 21-E could potentially be 
very close to the coast of Mexico as a hurricane over the weekend, 
and several Google DeepMind Ensemble members show the cyclone making 
landfall in southwestern or west-central Mexico.  The NHC forecast 
closely follows the HFIP Corrected Consensus Approach (HCCA), and 
isn't too far off from the Google DeepMind Ensemble Mean.  
Confidence in the track forecast is low, especially beyond day 3.

The cyclone is forecast to traverse extremely favorable oceanic and 
atmospheric conditions for at least the next 3 to 4 days.  The NHC 
intensity forecast shows steady strengthening over the next 24 hours 
as the cyclone works to develop a convective core.  After that time, 
rapid intensification is forecast during the 24 to 72 h time window. 
The DTOPS in the ECMWF-based SHIPS guidance is forecasting a 74 
percent chance of a 65-kt increase over the next 72 h.  The NHC 
intensity forecast is even more aggressive and shows 85 knots of 
strengthening over the next 72 h.  The NHC forecast is slightly 
below the HCCA and GDMI in the short term, but very near the HCCA 
and GDMI beyond Day 2.  The HAFS-A and HAFS-B models strengthen the 
cyclone to a category 4, and 36 of the 50 Google DeepMind members 
make it a category 4 or stronger, so the NHC forecast will 
aggressively show this system reaching category 4 strength at days 3 
and 4.  Beyond day 4, the possibility of increasing vertical wind 
shear and land interaction could induce some weakening, and most of 
the guidance shows the system losing some intensity after day 4.  
Confidence in the intensity forecast is above average.  We are 
fairly confident it will become a strong hurricane due to the 
favorable environmental conditions and model guidance.
