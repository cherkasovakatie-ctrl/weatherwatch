Tropical Storm Trudy Discussion Number   6
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
300 PM CST Wed Oct 14 2026
 
Trudy continues to become better organized, with the central 
convective mass becoming larger and colder.  Recent SAR, ASCAT and 
SSMIS passes give higher confidence that the center position is now 
embedded underneath the middle of the impressive-looking convective 
overcast.  The latest TAFB intensity estimate is 4.0/65 kt, and 
objective estimates from UW-CIMSS have been increasing, now in the 
44-51 kt range.  The ASCAT-B pass from around 16Z had a peak wind 
retrieval of 51 kt.  Based on the above data, the initial intensity 
is conservatively set at 55 kt.
 
Based on microwave center fixes over the past 6 to 12 hours, Trudy 
has been moving west-northwestward, or 300/8 kt, to the south of a 
mid-level ridge that extends over central Mexico. However, the ridge 
is expected to weaken on Thursday, as Trudy moves closer to 
Hurricane Simon.  The two tropical cyclones are forecast to be 
within 500 miles of each other by Thursday evening, which could be 
close enough to influence each other's steering.  By Thursday, an 
amplifying mid- to upper-level trough in the west-central Gulf of 
America and this possible interaction with Simon should cause Trudy 
to turn north-northwestward and possibly northward for a time 
Thursday night or Friday.  As Simon finally moves northwest late in 
the forecast period, it should allow the ridge to build back in and 
induce Trudy to make a leftward turn back toward the northwest. 
Since Simon is a small tropical cyclone though, it may not have much 
of an influence on Trudy's track, which could allow Trudy to turn 
west-northwestward and away from the coast of Mexico by the end of 
the forecast period.  The latest NHC track forecast is similar to 
the previous one through 96 hours, but is farther south at day 5, 
indicating a sharper turn toward the west-northwest or west by the 
end of the forecast period.

Environmental conditions continue to look quite favorable along 
Trudy's track through the next 60 hours, although recent NESDIS 
glider observations suggest that Trudy may briefly traverse lower 
ocean heat content for 24 hours between this evening and Thursday 
evening before reaching higher ocean heat content again.  The wind 
shear will remain low through about 60 hours, but the ECMWF-based 
SHIPS guidance shows southwesterly vertical wind shear around 20 kt 
from day 3 to 5.  The NHC intensity forecast is similar to the 
previous one, calling for steady to rapid intensification over the 
next couple of days, peaking around 115 kt around hour 60. This is 
similar to the HCCA, GDMI, HAFS-A, and HAFS-B, all of which peak 
Trudy as a major hurricane between 105-120 kt in about 60 hours.  
After that time, increasing wind shear and drier air should cause 
steady weakening.  The NHC intensity forecast remains near the high 
end of the overall model guidance suite.
 
NHC continues to call for Trudy to get close to the coast of Mexico 
as a major hurricane, and given the track uncertainty, residents of 
southwestern and west-central Mexico should continue to closely 
monitor the progress of Trudy, and be ready to possibly implement 
their hurricane plan.
 
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Trudy is forecast to be a major hurricane as it approaches the
coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico late this week and
into the weekend. There is an increasing risk of wind, rain, and
storm surge impacts, and watches could be required for a portion of
these areas later today or tonight.

2. Large swells will likely begin to affect portions of the coast of 
southwestern Mexico late this week and continue through the weekend. 
These swells could cause life-threatening surf and rip current 
conditions.
