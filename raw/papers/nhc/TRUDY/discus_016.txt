Hurricane Trudy Discussion Number  16
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
300 AM CST Sat Oct 17 2026
 
Trudy's satellite presentation has changed little overall during 
the past several hours, although the curved band of deep convection 
wrapping around the northern semicircle has expanded somewhat, with 
cloud tops as cold as -85 degrees C. A 0053 UTC RCM-3 synthetic 
aperture radar (SAR) pass and a 0439 UTC Metop-B scatterometer pass 
helped refine the initial position and wind radii of the hurricane. 
The latest subjective Dvorak intensity estimate from TAFB and 
objective satellite estimates remain near 75 kt. Given the 
relatively steady intensity estimates and only modest changes in the 
convective organization, the initial intensity is held at 80 kt.
 
The initial motion is northwestward, or 325/7 kt. A continued 
northwestward motion at a slightly faster forward speed is expected 
during the next day or so as a weak low- to mid-level ridge builds 
over eastern Mexico. This general motion should continue through 
Sunday, bringing the center of Trudy very close to the coast of 
west-central Mexico. Thereafter, as Trudy weakens and becomes 
increasingly influenced by the low-level steering flow, a turn 
toward the west-northwest is expected, taking the cyclone away from 
the coast early next week. The latest NHC track forecast has been 
adjusted slightly to the left of the previous prediction, but 
remains along the right side of the track guidance envelope during 
the time of closest approach to the coast of Mexico.
 
While Trudy remains over warm waters with relatively high ocean heat 
content, some additional strengthening is possible today. However, 
moderate vertical wind shear, estimated at around 14 kt by the 
latest UW-CIMSS analysis, along with possible dry-air entrainment 
and the cyclone's proximity to the coast of Mexico, could limit 
further intensification. By late Sunday, increasing southwesterly 
vertical wind shear and a progressively drier mid-level environment 
are expected to induce steady weakening. The latest SHIPS guidance 
indicates that shear could increase to around 20 to 25 kt by early 
next week, while mid-level relative humidity decreases to below 50 
percent. These increasingly hostile conditions should eventually 
cause Trudy to lose its organized deep convection, with the cyclone 
forecast to become a post-tropical remnant low by day 4 and 
dissipate by day 5. The latest NHC intensity forecast is very 
similar to the previous prediction, lying near the upper end of the 
guidance envelope. 
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Trudy could strengthen some during the next 12 hours as it 
approaches the coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico this 
weekend, bringing an increasing risk of storm surge, damaging winds, 
and flooding rain. Hurricane Warnings and Watches, as well as 
Tropical Storm Warnings and Watches, are currently in effect for 
portions of the coast, and additional watches and warnings may be 
required.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Trudy will impact the southwest 
Mexican coast between Guerrero and Nayarit through the weekend. This 
rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides along 
and near the southwest Mexican coast in Michoacán, Colima, and 
Jalisco, especially in areas along steep terrain.
 
3. Large swells are affecting portions of the coast of southwestern
Mexico and will spread northward through the weekend, causing
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
