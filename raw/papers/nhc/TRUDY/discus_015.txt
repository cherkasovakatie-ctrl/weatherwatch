Hurricane Trudy Discussion Number  15
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
900 PM CST Fri Oct 16 2026
 
Trudy's satellite presentation has improved somewhat this evening, 
with deep convection becoming better organized near the center and a 
possible ragged eye beginning to reappear. Cloud tops as cold as -85 
degrees C have been observed within the convective band wrapping 
into the system over the northern semicircle. The latest subjective 
Dvorak intensity estimate from TAFB is T4.5/77 kt, while recent 
objective estimates have been as high as 75 kt. Given the recent 
improvement in the satellite presentation, the initial intensity has 
been increased to 80 kt.

The initial motion is northward, or about 350/6 kt. A turn toward 
the north-northwest and then northwest at a faster forward speed is 
expected overnight and into Saturday as a weak low- to mid-level 
ridge builds over eastern Mexico. This general motion should 
continue through Sunday, bringing the center of Trudy very close 
to portions of the coast of west-central Mexico. Thereafter, as 
Trudy weakens and becomes increasingly influenced by the low-level 
steering flow, a turn toward the west-northwest is anticipated, 
taking the cyclone away from the coast early next week. The latest 
NHC track forecast has been adjusted slightly to the left of the 
previous prediction, toward the HCCA, GDMI, and TVCN consensus aids, 
but remains on the right side of the track guidance envelope, closer 
to the coast of Mexico.

Some additional strengthening is forecast during the next 24 h as 
Trudy moves over warmer waters with increasing ocean heat content, 
away from the relatively cool wakes of previous tropical cyclones. 
However, possible dry-air entrainment and the cyclone's increasing 
proximity to the coast of Mexico could limit strengthening. Beyond 
36 to 48 h, increasing southwesterly vertical wind shear and a 
progressively drier mid-level environment should induce steady 
weakening. These increasingly hostile conditions are expected to 
cause Trudy to lose its organized deep convection, with the cyclone 
forecast to become a post-tropical remnant low by day 4 and 
dissipate by day 5. The latest NHC intensity forecast is very 
similar to the previous prediction, but has been nudged slightly 
higher between 12 and 24 h to account for the recent improvement in 
Trudy's satellite presentation and the increasingly favorable 
oceanic conditions.

 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Trudy is forecast to strengthen during the next 24 hours as it
approaches the coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico this
weekend, bringing an increasing risk of storm surge, damaging winds,
and flooding rain. Hurricane Warnings and Watches, as well as
Tropical Storm Warnings and Watches, are currently in effect for
portions of the coast, and additional watches and warnings may be
required.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Trudy will impact the southwest
Mexican coast between Guerrero and Nayarit through the weekend. This
rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides along 
and near the southwest Mexican coast in Michoacán, Colima, and 
Jalisco, especially in areas along steep terrain.
 
3. Large swells are affecting portions of the coast of southwestern
Mexico and will spread northward through the weekend, causing
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
