Hurricane Trudy Discussion Number  17
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
900 AM CST Sat Oct 17 2026

Trudy is slowly becoming better organized. The hurricane is 
producing a large central dense overcast with cloud tops colder than 
-80C, and curved bands of overshooting tops in the CDO indicate an 
eyewall is present underneath it. The various satellite intensity 
estimates are starting to increase, but not enough yet to support 
raising the intensity. Thus, the initial intensity remains 80 kt.

The hurricane is moving a little faster toward the northwest, 325/9 
kt. A continued northwestward motion is expected during the next day 
or so as a weak low- to mid-level ridge builds over eastern Mexico. 
This general motion should continue for the next 36 h or so, 
bringing the center of Trudy very close to the coast of west-central 
Mexico. Thereafter, as Trudy weakens and becomes steered mainly by 
low-level flow, a turn toward the west and west-northwest is 
expected, taking the cyclone away from the coast. The new track 
forecast has only minor adjustments from the previous forecast, with 
the center making its closest approach to the coast of Mexico around 
36-48 h.

Now that Trudy is over water with a higher oceanic heat content, 
some strengthening is possible. However, moderate vertical shear, 
dry air entrainment, and increasing land interaction could limit how 
much strengthening may occur. After 24 h or so, increasing 
southwesterly shear and decreasing mid-level moisture near the 
cyclone are likely to cause steady to rapid weakening even when the 
center would be moving away from the coast of Mexico. Current 
expectations are that the system will stop producing convection 
between 72-96 h and decay to a remnant low pressure, and the global 
models forecast the circulation to weaken to a trough by 120 h. The 
new intensity forecast has some tweaks from the previous forecast, 
and it lies near the upper edge of the intensity guidance envelope.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Trudy could strengthen some during the next 12 hours or so as it 
approaches the coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico this 
weekend, bringing an increasing risk of storm surge, damaging winds, 
and flooding rain. Hurricane Warnings and Watches, as well as 
Tropical Storm Warnings and Watches, are currently in effect for 
portions of the coast, and additional watches and warnings may be 
required.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Trudy will impact the southwest 
Mexican coast between Guerrero and Nayarit through the weekend. This 
rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides this 
weekend along and near the southwest Mexican coast in Michoacán, 
Colima, and Jalisco, especially in areas along steep terrain.
 
3. Large swells are affecting portions of the coast of southwestern
Mexico and will spread northward through the weekend, causing
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
