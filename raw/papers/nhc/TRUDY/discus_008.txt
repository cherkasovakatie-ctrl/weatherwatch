Hurricane Trudy Discussion Number   8
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP212026
300 AM CST Thu Oct 15 2026
 
Trudy's satellite presentation continues to improve, with an 
expanding central dense overcast containing cloud tops as cold as 
-85 degrees C and a curved convective band wrapping into the 
circulation from the north. Earlier microwave passes revealed 
improving inner-core organization, while a 0339 UTC MetOp-B 
scatterometer pass helped refine the cyclone's wind radii and 
structure. The latest subjective Dvorak intensity estimate from TAFB 
is T4.0/65 kt, which is consistent with the latest objective 
estimates. Based on these data, the initial intensity is increased 
to 65 kt, making Trudy a category 1 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson 
Hurricane Wind Scale.

The initial motion is northwestward, or 305/8 kt. A gradual turn 
toward the north-northwest is expected during the next day or so as 
the mid-level ridge extending across central Mexico weakens in 
response to an amplifying mid- to upper-level trough over the Gulf 
of America. Some interaction with Tropical Storm Simon, a compact 
tropical cyclone located several hundred miles to the northwest, 
could also influence Trudy's steering. This pattern is expected to 
bring Trudy very close to the coast of southwestern and west-central 
Mexico over the weekend, from near Punta San Telmo to Cabo 
Corrientes, before a rebuilding ridge to the north induces a 
westward turn away from the coast early next week. Considerable 
track uncertainty remains, particularly from days 4 to 5, when the 
guidance continues to show a significant amount of cross-track 
spread. The latest NHC track forecast is essentially unchanged from 
the previous prediction through 72 h, with another slight adjustment 
to the north at days 4 and 5 as Trudy turns westward away from the 
coast. The forecast closely follows the latest HCCA and GDMI 
consensus aids.

Environmental conditions remain quite favorable for significant 
strengthening during the next couple of days, with very low vertical 
wind shear, warm sea surface temperatures, and plenty of deep 
moisture surrounding Trudy. The continued improvement in Trudy's 
convective structure and inner-core organization suggests that the 
hurricane could be poised to undergo a period of rapid 
intensification (RI). This possibility is supported by the latest 
SHIPS RI guidance, which indicates a 28 to 40 percent probability of 
a 35-kt increase in maximum winds during the next 24 h, roughly four 
to six times the climatological mean. Although Trudy could briefly 
encounter lower ocean heat content associated with the wakes of 
previous tropical cyclones, oceanic conditions are forecast to 
become more favorable thereafter. The NHC intensity forecast calls 
for RI during the next 24 h, with Trudy expected to become a major 
hurricane by Friday, followed by additional strengthening through 
Saturday. By day 3, increasing vertical wind shear and the 
entrainment of drier mid-level air are expected to induce weakening. 
The NHC intensity forecast remains above the guidance envelope 
during the next few days, then lies near the upper end of the 
guidance envelope thereafter.

Trudy is forecast to approach the coast of southwestern and 
west-central Mexico as a major hurricane this weekend. 
Tropical-storm-force winds could reach portions of the coast as 
early as Saturday, and watches will likely be required for portions 
of the coast later this morning. Interests along the coast of 
southwestern and west-central Mexico should closely monitor the 
progress of Trudy and be prepared to implement their hurricane 
plans.


KEY MESSAGES:

1. Trudy is forecast to be a major hurricane as it approaches the 
coast of southwestern and west-central Mexico this weekend. There is 
an increasing risk of wind, rain, and storm surge impacts, and 
watches will likely be required for portions of the coast later this 
morning.

2. Heavy rainfall associated with Trudy will impact the southwest 
coast of Mexico between Guerrero and Jalisco through the weekend. 
This rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides by 
the weekend across Colima and Jalisco, especially in areas along 
steep terrain.

3. Large swells will begin to affect portions of the coast of 
southwestern Mexico Friday and continue through the weekend. These 
swells could cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.
