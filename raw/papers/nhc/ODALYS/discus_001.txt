Tropical Storm Odalys Discussion Number   1
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP162026
200 AM PDT Sun Sep 20 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that the convective organization of 
Odalys has improved over the past several hours. Deep convection 
continues to develop within a band wrapping around the western and 
southern semicircles, with a recent burst of convection expanding 
over or near the circulation center. The latest subjective Dvorak 
intensity estimate from TAFB is T2.5/35 kt. Given these data and the 
improved satellite presentation, advisories are being initiated on 
newly formed Tropical Storm Odalys, with an initial intensity of 35 
kt.

The initial motion is estimated to be northwestward, or 305/7 kt. 
This general motion is expected to continue during the next day or 
two as a low- to mid-level subtropical ridge remains positioned to 
the north. Thereafter, Odalys is forecast to slow considerably and 
turn sharply eastward by Tuesday, then east-northeastward through 
the latter part of the week in response to a developing weakness in 
the ridge as an upper-level trough deepens over the western United 
States. Although the track guidance remains in decent agreement 
through the first half of the week, increasing cross-track spread 
develops thereafter. This appears to be due, at least in part, to 
differences in how the models handle the evolution of the 
upper-level trough over the western United States and the potential 
influence of another developing tropical cyclone tracking 
west-northwestward offshore of southwestern Mexico. These factors 
lead to lower confidence in the track forecast during the latter 
portion of the forecast period. The NHC track forecast closely 
follows the HCCA and GDMI aids through around day 4, then reflects 
more of a blend of the two thereafter as the spread increases.

Environmental conditions appear favorable for gradual strengthening 
during the next couple of days, with warm sea surface temperatures 
and plenty of deep moisture expected along the forecast track. 
However, modest north-northeasterly vertical wind shear may temper 
the rate of intensification during the next 24 h. Thereafter, the 
rate of strengthening could increase as the shear relaxes and Odalys 
remains in a favorable environment. The NHC forecast has the system 
becoming a hurricane by Monday, with some additional strengthening 
possible through Tuesday. Thereafter, progressively drier mid-level 
air, along with increasing vertical wind shear anticipated later in 
the forecast period, should induce a steady weakening trend. There 
is additional uncertainty in the intensity forecast due to the 
potential influence of another tropical cyclone evolving well to the 
east, which could affect its intensity depending on the eventual 
tracks and proximity of the two systems late in the week. The NHC 
intensity forecast lies near the middle to upper end of the guidance 
envelope through much of the period.
