Recent satellite imagery continues to show a ragged eye feature 
embedded within an area of increased deep convection, with cloud-top 
temperatures near -80 C mainly north and west of the center. 
Subjective Dvorak intensity estimates from TAFB came in at T 5.0/90 
kt, with UW-CIMSS objective satellite intensity estimates ranging 
from 85-112 kt. Using a blend of this data, the initial intensity is 
raised to 95 kt.
 
The initial motion is estimated to be east-northeastward, or 060/8
kt. Odalys is being steered by a mid- to upper-level trough to its
northwest. The models generally agree that the hurricane should
gradually turn toward the northeast and north over the next couple
of days. As the hurricane begins its northward turn, forecast
confidence in the track decreases. The system is expected to weaken
and become increasingly shallow, making it susceptible to steering
from lower-level winds while potentially interacting with the outer
circulation of Hurricane Polo. Consequently, there is considerable
spread in model guidance.  However, the general consensus is for a
slower forward speed and a southward component of motion after day
3. The updated NHC track forecast closely follows the previous
advisory and lies near the reliable HCCA and GDMI forecast guidance.
 
In the near term, some intensity fluctuations are possible as the 
improved structure of Odalys could allow for additional 
strengthening despite moderate shear and the potential for intrusion 
of drier air from the west. Thereafter, guidance suggests a faster 
rate of weakening after 36 h as the environment becomes more stable 
with the increase of vertical wind shear and mid-level dry air, 
along with decreasing SSTs. These conditions will likely make it 
difficult for Odalys to sustain organized convection later this 
weekend, and the updated forecast shows Odalys as a post-tropical 
cyclone at 72 h based on the latest GFS and ECMWF simulated 
satellite imagery.
 
Moisture from both Odalys and Polo will be carried a long distance
into the southwestern U.S. this weekend and early next week, and may
indirectly contribute to a heavy rainfall and flash flood threat in
the region. See products issued by the Weather Prediction Center for
more information.
