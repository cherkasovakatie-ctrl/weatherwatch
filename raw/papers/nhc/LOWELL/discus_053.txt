Tropical Storm Lowell Discussion Number  53
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM HST Tue Sep 08 2026
 
Proxy-visible satellite and water vapor imagery show that Lowell has 
continued to deteriorate as the cyclone has been adversely affected 
by dry air and wind shear. The center of the cyclone has moved 
underneath an area of deep convection that has formed recently, 
however the bulk of the convection is on the northern side of the 
circulation. Recent subjective Dvorak intensity estimates are 45-55 
kt, while the objective intensity estimates are lower in the range 
of 41-46 kt. Since earlier scatterometer data showed stronger winds 
than suggested by the satellite estimates, the initial intensity is 
only lowered to 55 kt for this advisory.
 
Lowell is currently moving northward around 12 kt, as the storm is 
being guided by a mid-level low to the northwest and a ridge from 
the north-northeast. On Wednesday, a more gradual shift towards the 
northwest should occur as high pressure builds in from the north. 
The storm is then expected to lose speed on Thursday as the cyclone 
becomes more shallow and lower-level flow becomes more dominant. 
There is a good degree of uncertainty in the track around 72 h, as 
Lowell approaches another upper-level low from the northwest. The 
guidance generally has the cyclone turning to the north or 
north-northwest around Friday and into the weekend. There is a large 
spread in the guidance about the timing and how far north the storm 
will go. Given the spread in the track models, there is a higher 
than normal uncertainty in the long-range NHC track forecast. This 
forecast is similar to the previous prediction, but nudged to the 
right based on the latest consensus aids.

Lowell is expected to remain in a hostile environment with moderate 
wind shear and dry air, thus a gradual weakening trend is expected 
to occur. The simulated satellite imagery from the GFS and ECMWF 
indicate that Lowell could lose most of its deep convection by 
Thursday, and potentially become a post-tropical cyclone on Friday. 
However, the previously mentioned second upper-level low could help 
Lowell build some deep convection late Friday into Saturday. This in 
turn could cause some slight strengthening of the storm, but there 
is uncertainty regarding the stucture of Lowell at that time. 
Therefore, there is a good degree of uncertainty with this portion 
of the intensity forecast. The current NHC intensity forecast is 
similar to the previous prediction and calls for Lowell to become a 
post-tropical remnant low around day 5. 

 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Post-storm safety: Follow advice of local officials and avoid
areas of downed power lines and flooding. Ensure generators are
properly ventilated and placed outside at least 20 feet away from
dwellings and garages to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning. During
clean up, be careful when using chainsaws and power tools. Drink
plenty of water to avoid heat exhaustion.
 
2. Large waves will continue to produce life-threatening surf and
rip current conditions during the next day or so along exposed
coasts of the Hawaiian Islands.
