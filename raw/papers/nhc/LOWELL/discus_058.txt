Tropical Storm Lowell Discussion Number  58
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
500 AM HST Thu Sep 10 2026


Infrared satellite imagery over the last several hours suggests
that the convection over Lowell's northeastern quadrant is
degrading. Lowell's low-level center is also becoming increasingly
displaced from this convection. The last subjective Dvorak
intensity estimates from HFO and JTWC both rated Lowell at 2.0/2.0
or 30 kt, however scatterometer data from 0700 UTC depicted max
winds closer to 55 kt. With Lowell's convection decreasing, the
initial intensity was lowered slightly to 50 kt.
 
The initial motion was estimated at 270/13 kt. Lowell's westward 
motion continues to be steered by a mid-level high centered to its 
north-northeast. Heading into the weekend, Lowell is forecast to 
turn northwestward as it interacts with a cutoff low over the 
north-central Pacific. After which, the storm will become wedged 
between two mid-level highs, greatly inhibiting its forward motion. 
The track models exhibit a wide spread in Lowell's motion beyond 36 
h, with global models suggesting that the storm will more or less 
remain stationary over the open central Pacific when it is caught 
between the aforementioned mid-level highs. Because of this, little 
was changed in the official track forecast, save for minor leftward 
adjustments within the first 36 h given Lowell's due westward 
initial motion.

Lowell is expected to remain in a hostile environment characterized 
by moderate vertical wind shear, dry mid-level air, and marginal 
SSTs. In fact, simulated satellite imagery from the GFS and the 
ECMWF suggest that Lowell could become post-tropical within the next 
24 h. The main complication, however, is that Lowell interacting 
with the aforementioned cutoff low could work to re-invigorate 
convection heading into the weekend. Because of this, forecasting 
Lowell's intensity and post-tropical transition is difficult, as the 
storm's environment favors steady weakening, but another flare-up of 
convection from this cutoff low could keep the intensity steady 
through the weekend. Therefore, Lowell's intensity forecast was kept 
relatively steady through the first 48 h, followed by weakening late 
this weekend into early next week. Lowell is ultimately anticipated 
to dissipate after interacting with a frontal system early next 
week. 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Post-storm safety: Follow advice of local officials and avoid
areas of downed power lines and flooding. Ensure generators are
properly ventilated and placed outside at least 20 feet away from
dwellings and garages to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning. During
clean up, be careful when using chainsaws and power tools. Drink
plenty of water to avoid heat exhaustion.
