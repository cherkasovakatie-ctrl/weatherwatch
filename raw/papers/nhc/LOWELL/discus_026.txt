Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  26
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Wed Sep 02 2026
 
Lowell has continued to rapidly intensify over the Central Pacific 
today. The compact hurricane has maintained a very warm, circular 
eye with a diameter of 15 n mi for the past several hours. A 
symmetric ring of very deep convection surrounds the eye with cold 
cloud tops of -70 to -75 deg C. The subjective Dvorak and objective 
UW-CIMSS estimates have risen since the prior advisory, with a 
T7.0/140 kt classification from JTWC along with recent AiDT and 
D-PRINT estimates around 140 kt. Based on these developments, Lowell 
has become a Category 5 hurricane with an initial intensity of 140 
kt. A NOAA aircraft research mission is scheduled to collect data in 
Lowell later today.

Since the hurricane is moving over a region of very high oceanic 
heat content in a low shear environment, further near-term 
intensification remains possible. An eyewall replacement cycle 
(ERC) would be expected to commence in the near future, but the 
latest available passive microwave images did not suggest an ERC is 
underway. These favorable environmental and oceanic conditions are 
expected to persist for the next several days, meaning that Lowell 
is likely to remain a major hurricane while internal dynamic 
processes like ERCs modulate its intensity fluctuations. The NHC 
intensity forecast remains on the high side of the latest guidance 
and follows the multi-model consensus aids, trending closer to IVCN 
later in the period. There is high confidence that Lowell will 
remain a major hurricane through the weekend.

Lowell is moving just south of due west (265/9 kt). This motion is 
expected to continue with a gradually slower forward speed during 
the next few days as Lowell moves to the south of a mid-level ridge 
over the Central Pacific. A cutoff mid- to upper-level low is 
forecast to drop southwestward from the North Pacific and weaken the 
steering ridge this weekend. Then, a sharp turn toward the north or 
north-northeast is forecast at days 4-5 while Lowell interacts with 
this upper-level feature. There is large spread in the various 
models and ensemble solutions regarding how sharp of a turn occurs, 
but once again the overall trend has been an eastward shift in the 
long-range track envelope. Thus, the updated NHC track forecast 
was adjusted to the right of the previous prediction at 96-120 h.
