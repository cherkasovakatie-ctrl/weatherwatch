Hurricane Rachel Discussion Number  16
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP182026
800 PM MST Wed Sep 30 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that Rachel's convective structure has 
changed little over the past several hours. Deep convection 
continues to burst near the center, with cloud-top temperatures as 
cold as -85 C. The latest subjective Dvorak intensity estimate from 
TAFB is T4.5/77 kt, while objective estimates have ranged from 65 to 
85 kt. Given these estimates and the persistent deep convection, 
the initial intensity is increased to 80 kt.
 
Rachel continues to move northwestward, or 320/6 kt. A northwestward 
motion at a slower forward speed is expected during the next day or 
so as the cyclone moves along the southwestern periphery of a 
mid-level ridge while a mid- to upper-level trough remains over the 
southwestern United States and northwestern Mexico. As the trough 
lifts northeastward and weakens, a ridge is forecast to build north 
of Rachel, causing the system to gradually turn west-northwestward 
and then westward later this week. The track guidance remains in 
good agreement on this overall scenario, and the latest NHC track 
forecast is essentially an update of the previous prediction, lying 
near the HCCA and GDMI aids.
 
Environmental conditions should remain generally favorable for 
strengthening during the next couple of days, with very warm sea 
surface temperatures and plenty of mid-level moisture along the 
forecast track. Persistent easterly vertical wind shear of around 15 
to 20 kt may be a limiting factor in the short term, but the shear 
is forecast to gradually relax thereafter. Rachel could strengthen 
rapidly by Thursday, especially if a more persistent inner core 
becomes established, and is forecast to become a major hurricane 
within the next couple of days. Thereafter, progressively cooler sea 
surface temperatures and dry mid-level air should induce a gradual 
weakening trend. The latest NHC intensity forecast is similar to the 
previous advisory, lying above the guidance envelope in the short 
term before trending toward the upper end of the guidance 
thereafter.
 
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Although the core of Rachel is forecast to remain offshore of
Mexico, wind gusts of tropical-storm force are possible along
portions of the coast of southwestern Mexico through tonight.
 
2. Outer bands from Rachel will continue to produce locally heavy
rainfall across portions of coastal southwest Mexico. This rainfall
may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides, especially in
areas of steep terrain.
 
3. Large waves could produce life-threatening surf and rip current
conditions along the southwestern and west-central coast of Mexico
and the southern Baja California Peninsula during the next several
days.
