Hurricane Rachel Discussion Number  32
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP182026
800 PM MST Sun Oct 04 2026
 
The satellite presentation for Rachel has degraded over the last
several hours as the eye has filled in and the inner core seems to
have come apart as well. Based on water vapor imagery, it appears 
that dry air has managed to make its way into the core of the
hurricane. Using a combination of the most recent TAFB Dvorak fix
and objective intensity estimates from UW-CIMSS, the initial
intensity has been lowered to 85 kt. This level of intensity could
be generous given the structure of the hurricane at this time.
 
Rachel is moving west-northwestward around 6 kt. The high pressure 
system to the north of the cyclone should continue to steer it on a 
generally westward track through the middle of the week. Afterwards, 
the high should weaken and shift eastward as a trough cuts off close 
to southern California. This should cause Rachel to turn back to 
the west-northwest by the end of the week. The current NHC forecast 
track is very close to the previous one, with minor adjustments 
being made based on the HCCA and GDMI forecast aids. 

More fluctuations in intensity are possible overnight for Rachel. 
However, the cyclone is expected to enter a more drier environment 
as it moves farther westward and the sea-surface temperatures are 
expected to be 26C or less in a few days. Therefore, gradual 
weakening is expected to start sometime on Monday and continue 
through the forecast period. The current NHC intensity forecast 
shows a weakening trend similar to the previous one, however the
forecast has been adjusted based on the trends of the HCCA forecast 
aid.
