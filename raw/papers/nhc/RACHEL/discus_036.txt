Hurricane Rachel Discussion Number  36
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP182026
800 PM PDT Mon Oct 05 2026
 
The visible satellite presentation for Rachel has become far less
impressive than it was earlier today. The eye of the hurricane has
started to fill in again and the deep convection has become
asymmetric based on the infrared imagery. Using a combination of
the objective and subjective intensity estimates, the initial
intensity has been held at 80 kt for this advisory.

The hurricane is moving westward at 8 kt steered by a high pressure
system to the north-northeast of the cyclone. This area of high 
pressure is expected to keep Rachel moving generally westward to 
west-northwestward for the next several days. However, towards the 
end of the week, that high pressure system should weaken as a mid- 
to upper-level trough amplifies near the west coast of the United 
States. As a result, Rachel is expected to slow down and then turn 
northeastward at the end of the forecast period. The current NHC 
forecast track is similar to the previous track, but adjusted based 
on the HCCA and GDMI forecast aids.

The cyclone is expected to move into a region with drier air and 
significantly cooler sea-surface temperatures. Therefore, the 
current NHC intensity forecast has Rachel progressively weakening in 
a similar trend to the previous forecast. The intensity forecast is 
on the upper end of the guidance envelope, but is very close to the 
HCCA forecast aid.
