Hurricane Genevieve Discussion Number  25
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP072026
1100 PM HST Wed Jul 29 2026

The satellite presentation of Genevieve's convective structure has 
degraded since the previous advisory. The low-level circulation 
remains embedded beneath a central dense overcast with cloud-top 
temperatures as cold as -70 C, which have been steadily warming, 
likely due to increasing dry-air entrainment. Subjective Dvorak 
intensity estimates from TAFB and the latest objective estimates 
from UW-CIMSS range from 70 to 80 kt. Based on the satellite data 
and these estimates, the initial intensity is lowered to 80 kt. 

The initial motion estimate is 300/10 kt. Genevieve is expected to 
continue moving generally west-northwestward during the next several 
days as a low- to mid-level ridge builds north of the cyclone. A 
modest increase in forward speed is expected during the next couple 
of days as the ridge builds farther west. Thereafter, a gradual turn 
toward the northwest, then the north-northwest is forecast in 
response to a weakness forming in the subtropical ridge. The latest 
guidance remains in good agreement, and the updated NHC track 
forecast is essentially unchanged from the previous advisory.

Environmental conditions are forecast to become increasingly 
unfavorable during the next several days. Genevieve will move over 
progressively cooler sea surface temperatures while encountering 
increasing southwesterly shear and a drier mid-level environment. 
These factors should result in a steady weakening trend, with the 
cyclone forecast to weaken below hurricane strength by Friday. By 
this weekend, persistent dry air entrainment and a more stable 
environment should cause deep convection to gradually diminish, 
resulting in Genevieve becoming a remnant low by 96 h. The updated 
NHC intensity forecast reflects this weakening trend and is very 
close to the previous advisory.
