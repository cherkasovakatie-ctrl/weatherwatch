Tropical Depression Nine Discussion Number   1
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       AL092026
400 PM CDT Tue Oct 06 2026
 
Satellite imagery indicates that the area of low pressure located
over the southwestern Gulf of America has recently developed a
closed circulation, though it might still be elongated. Associated
showers and thunderstorms have also become better organized, and
this system now meets the criteria to be a tropical depression, the
ninth one of the season. The initial intensity is set at 30 kt based
on a Dvorak classification from TAFB.
 
The depression has barely moved over the past 6 to 12 hours, with 
the initial motion estimated to be 120/1 kt. The cyclone is expected 
to track slowly eastward or east-northeastward during the next 
couple of days while it moves in the flow near the base of a broad 
deep-layer trough, and could bring gusty winds and heavy rains to 
the western portions of the Yucatan Peninsula on Thursday. By 
Friday, a sharp turn to the northeast is expected when a shortwave 
trough forms over Texas and a ridge strengthens over the western 
Atlantic. This pattern change should cause the system to accelerate 
toward the northern Gulf Coast on Friday and into early Saturday. 
The NHC track forecast lies near the fast side of the guidance, in 
best agreement with the GDMI and ECAI aids. It should be noted that 
there is significant uncertainty in where the system makes the 
northeast turn and how fast it moves toward the coast. It appears 
that the stronger the system gets, the faster it will move toward 
the coast given the strength of the deep-layer flow. The NOAA G-IV 
jet is currently sampling the environment and special upper-air 
soundings are being deployed over the south-central U.S. The 
assimilation of these data into the dynamical models should improve 
their representation of the current environmental conditions, 
ideally yielding more reliable track and intensity guidance.
 
The environmental conditions appear generally conducive for
strengthening over the next few days, and given that the storm
appears compact, it will likely steadily strengthen during that
time. The NHC intensity forecast shows the system becoming a
tropical storm by early Wednesday and a hurricane on Thursday. Based
on the environmental conditions and the model guidance, the cyclone
will likely peak in strength before reaching the coast. Although not
seen in the forecast, some weakening seems likely when the storm
nears the Gulf Coast due to an increase in vertical wind shear, but
it is still expected to be a dangerous hurricane when it makes
landfall. This forecast is in fair agreement with the consensus
aids, but it should be noted that there are skillful models that are
higher than the current forecast.
 
Although the storm is expected to be compact for the next few days,
it will likely grow significantly in size, particularly on the
eastern side, when it reaches the northern Gulf Coast. Therefore,
based on the forecast uncertainty and expected larger size, users
are urged not to focus on the forecast points and stay tuned to
updates.
 
Key Messages:
 
1. There is an increasing risk of dangerous wind and storm surge 
impacts along portions of the northern Gulf Coast, and watches could 
be required on Wednesday. Residents from Louisiana to the Florida 
Panhandle should closely monitor the latest forecast updates and 
ensure they have their hurricane plan in place.
 
2. The system will bring areas of heavy rainfall to the central 
U.S. Gulf coast Friday into the weekend.  Flash and urban flooding, 
along with isolated river flooding, will be possible.
 
3. Yucatan Peninsula of Mexico: The depression is expected to
strengthen and could bring gusty winds and areas of heavy rain to
the western portion of the Yucatan Peninsula in a couple of days.
