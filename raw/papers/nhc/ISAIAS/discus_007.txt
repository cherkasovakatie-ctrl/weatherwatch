Right after the previous advisory, Air Force Reserve hurricane 
hunter aircraft launched a dropsonde in the southwest quadrant of 
the storm, finding a 150 m boundary layer average wind of 76 kt, 
which was the basis for upgrading Isaias to a hurricane at 0330 UTC. 
Since that time, a NOAA-P3 aircraft has taken over the in-situ 
sampling of the hurricane, finding that Isaias continues to 
intensify. The Tail Doppler Radar (TDR) swaths still indicate some 
northward tilt with height, likely an impact of moderate westerly 
vertical wind shear that continues to impinge on the circulation, 
though the circulation is more aligned than in yesterday's mission. 
The peak TDR winds were up to 80 kt on the 500 m level, and 
experimental data that reduces the peak TDR winds to the 10 meter 
level support a maximum sustained wind of 70 kt this advisory.
 
Aircraft fixes indicate that Isaias continues to maintain a
east-northeastward heading at 060/8 kt. The most critical steering
feature is a subtropical ridge centered east of the Bahamas that is
building westward, leading to an enhancement of environmental
southerly flow over the eastern Gulf. At the same time, a mid-level
trough stretched across the northern Gulf coast will be reinforced
by another impulse digging in from the Rocky Mountains, leading to a
more amplified short-wave over east Texas. The net effect of these
synoptic features should result in Isaias making a turn to the
northeast and the north over the next 24-48 h. The guidance this
cycle has not changed appreciably since the previous cycle, and
there continue to be across-track differences between the further
west physics-based models, and further east AI/Machine
Learning-based models. The NHC track forecast remains very close to
the prior one, and continues to lead more on the Google DeepMind
ensemble mean (GDMI) and HFIP Corrected Consensus Approach (HCCA)
track aids, which have been our best performing track guidance this
year.  As mentioned previously, after Isaias moves inland, the
guidance is showing a more subtle westward shift as Isaias is
captured by the short wave trough to its west, leading in the
formation of a potent cutoff low over the Tennessee valley as the
tropical cyclone becomes post-tropical.
 
The intensity forecast continues to be somewhat tricky. Isaias has 
been trying to become better vertically aligned despite dealing with 
around 20 kt of westerly vertical wind shear, and an eyewall keeps 
coming and going in aircraft fixes. Other environmental factors (30 
C sea-surface temperatures, sufficient deep-layer moisture) are 
quite favorable. Because the shear is not forecast to increase 
appreciably for the next 24 h, and because the hurricane has been 
intensifying despite the shear, significant intensification is still 
shown in the short-term, making Isaias just shy of major hurricane 
intensity in 24 h. This part of the intensity forecast is on the 
higher end of the guidance, close to the HCCA and GDMI intensity 
aids. In the final 12-24 h before Isaias makes landfall on the north 
Gulf coast, vertical wind shear ramps up to 40-50 kt, and this will 
likely impart some weakening. However, at the same time, the 
hurricane's wind field may become broader and more asymmetric as it 
begins to interact with the short-wave trough capturing it from the 
west. Regardless, Isaias is expected to remain a dangerous hurricane 
through landfall, and gusty winds are expected well inland after 
landfall given the anticipated expansion of the wind field and 
accelerating forward speed of the storm.
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Isaias is expected to strengthen and be a strong hurricane when
it approaches the northern Gulf Coast on Friday. A life-threatening
storm surge is expected along a portion of the northern Gulf Coast,
and a Storm Surge Warning remains in effect from the Mouth of the
Mississippi River to Steinhatchee, Florida. Residents in the warning
area should follow advice given by local officials and evacuate if
told to do so.
 
2. Damaging hurricane force-winds are expected over portions of the
Florida Panhandle, southern Alabama, and southern Mississippi, and a
Hurricane Warning remains in effect from Ocean Springs, Mississippi
to the Bay/Gulf County Line, Florida. Residents in these areas
should prepare for the possibility of long-duration power outages.
 
3. Isaias will bring areas of heavy rainfall to the central U.S.
Gulf coast and much of the Southeast Friday into the weekend. Flash
and urban flooding, along with isolated river flooding, will be
possible.
 
4. Isaias could bring gusty winds and areas of heavy rain to the
western portion of the Yucatan Peninsula through today.
