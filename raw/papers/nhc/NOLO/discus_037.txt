Hurricane Nolo Discussion Number  37
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP152026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Tue Sep 29 2026
 
Nolo's satellite appearance continues to degrade this morning as it 
succumbs to strong vertical wind shear. Drier air has also started 
to entrain into the inner core of the system, eroding southern 
portions of the circulation and causing cloud tops to warm. 
Satellite intensity estimates continue to decrease, with the latest 
Current Intensity (CI) estimates from JTWC and UW-CIMSS ranging 
between 75 and 90 kt. Given the degraded satellite presentation and 
these estimates, the initial intensity is lowered to 85 kt for this 
advisory.

Nolo has continued to slow down and is moving northward, or 360/7 
kt. A continued northward motion with a decreasing forward speed is 
expected through tonight. In about 24 h, the system is forecast to 
decelerate further as it interacts with a mid- to upper-level 
trough. Following this interaction, a mid-level ridge is expected to 
build to the northwest of Nolo, resulting in a sharp westward turn. 
There continue to be along-track differences among the track 
guidance. The better-performing HCCA and Google DeepMind (GDMI) 
models are slower than the global models and depict a much slower 
turn and subsequent re-acceleration. Toward the end of the forecast 
period, Nolo is expected to move generally westward across the 
Papahanaumokuakea Marine National Monument. The latest NHC track 
forecast has once again been slowed down through 72 h and trends 
toward the latest HCCA and GDMI aids.

The moderate-to-strong vertical wind shear is expected to continue 
impacting Nolo for the next 36-48 h or so, with dry mid-level air 
also entraining into the circulation. This should result in a steady 
weakening trend, and Nolo is forecast to become a tropical storm 
during that time. The shear is expected to relax in 48 to 60 h. 
Although the system remains over warm sea surface temperatures, the 
dry mid-level environment may inhibit the rate of re-strengthening. 
The latest NHC intensity forecast follows these trends, showing 
steady weakening in the short term before the system re-strengthens 
into a hurricane by the end of the forecast period. This prediction 
lies near the middle of the guidance envelope.
