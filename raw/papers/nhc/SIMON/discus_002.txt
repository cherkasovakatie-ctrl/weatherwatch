Tropical Storm Simon Discussion Number   2
NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL       EP202026
300 PM CST Wed Oct 07 2026

The system continues to gradually become better organized on 
satellite imagery with convective banding features becoming more 
prominent.  The latest subjective Dvorak classification from TAFB 
had a T-number of 2.5, corresponding to an intensity of 35 kt.  On 
this basis, the cyclone is being upgraded to Tropical Storm Simon 
for this advisory.  The storm has developed upper-level anticyclonic 
outflow over all quadrants of the circulation.

ASCAT-B scatterometer data suggested that the center of the cyclone 
is a little north of the previous track, but the overall motion 
still appears to be toward the west-northwest, or about 300/7 kt.  
Simon is likely to continue this general west-northwestward motion 
for the next day or so, to the south of a weak mid-level ridge.  By 
Friday, the system should turn northwestward and then northward 
ahead of a 500 mb trough approaching Baja California. In 3-5 days, 
the cyclone should move generally northward between a mid-level 
anticyclone and the trough near Baja California.  The official 
forecast track remains on the right side of the guidance envelope, 
and is close to the Google DeepMind ensemble mean.
 
For the next few days, Simon is expected to remain within an 
environment of low vertical wind shear and moist low- to mid-level 
air, while moving over waters of high oceanic heat content.  These 
factors should result in a significant increase in intensity this 
week, and the system is likely to strengthen into a hurricane, 
and then into a major hurricane later this week.  The NHC intensity 
forecast is a little above the intensity model consensus, IVCN.

Key Messages:

1. Simon is expected to strengthen into a hurricane by Thursday 
night and approach the west-central coast of Mexico as a major 
hurricane on Saturday. 

2. There is a risk of dangerous wind and storm surge impacts along 
portions of the west-central coast of Mexico over the weekend and 
into early next week, and watches could be required by tonight.

3. Heavy rainfall associated with Simon will impact portions of 
southwestern Mexico later this week and into the weekend. This 
rainfall may produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides, 
especially in areas of steep terrain.

4. Swells will affect portions of the southwestern and west-central 
coast of Mexico through the weekend.  These swells are likely to 
cause life-threatening surf and rip current conditions.  Please 
consult products from your local weather office.
