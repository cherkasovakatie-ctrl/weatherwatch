Status at the cutoff (verified 18 Oct 2026)

Observed. NOAA CPC issued an El Niño Advisory (8 Oct 2026). September relative indices: Niño 3.4 +2.1 °C, Niño 3 +3.0 °C, Niño 1+2 +3.9 °C. RONI JAS 2026 +1.69 (traditional ONI +2.16). Weekly OISST for the week centred 7 Oct: Niño 3.4 +3.3 °C (relative +2.4). Upper-300 m heat content (180°–100°W) +3.39 °C in September, the highest since the record began in 1979. Low-level westerly anomalies, enhanced convection from the central to the eastern Pacific and significantly negative traditional and equatorial SOI show the ocean and atmosphere are coupled.

Forecast (not observation). CPC: El Niño to strengthen through the end of 2026, with a greater than 83% chance of continuing through January–March 2027; chance of a historic event (3-month RONI at or above +2.5 °C) 54% for SON, 83% for OND and 70% for NDJ. IRI (21 Sep 2026): objective model probabilities of El Niño 100% from SON 2026 to FMA 2027.

Premise check. "Very strong" is a forecast on the official RONI, not yet an observation. On the traditional ONI, JAS 2026 is already the highest JAS value since 1950.

Sources: CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion; IRI ENSO forecast; CPC index files in the data archive. The Australian Bureau of Meteorology page available on 18 Oct carried data only to 9 Aug 2026 and was not used.

Planned scope

This paper will follow the same framework as Paper 1: preconditions, triggers, amplifiers, suppressors, feedbacks and consequences, each graded A–D on event-specific evidence, with a timeline, an evidence table and verified references. RONI is the primary index.

Questions specific to this event:

  • Explosive growth from a weak La Niña and the record subsurface heat content.
  • Why the event is so strongly east-based (Niño 1+2 far ahead of Niño 4).
  • The negative PDO and the North Pacific marine heatwaves: amplifier, suppressor or coincidence?
  • Observations, forecasts and conditional scenarios kept strictly separate.
